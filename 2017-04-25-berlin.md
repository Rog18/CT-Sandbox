I have spent my previous couple of weeks living in Berlin. From chilly March to cold April, I have got the chance to visit some neighborhoods of the city and avail public transport. To say the least about my first impressions of the city, Berlin is big and lot of other things that I am yet to know. I have seen it becoming warmer and more colorful. I saw my first (and a few more!) snowfall here just minutes after the sun shone gloriously. Berlin can also surprise you with its unruffled public transport. Or how it is — in your face historic and cultural yet cosmopolitan.

I am mainstream on most of the days so I started with its primary sights. I walked along the remains of the Berlin Wall (Documentation Centre and East Side Gallery), visited Checkpoint Charlie, Holocaust memorial, Brandenburg gate and the Reichstag. Most of the places I just mentioned happen to be in the central borough of Berlin except the East Side Gallery. I was especially intrigued by Checkpoint Charlie where the history of Berlin is very well preserved and comes across as tangible. A pavilion called Black Box is installed at Checkpoint Charlie that consists of literature, media, big photos and erected chunks of Berlin Wall. All of that tells you how the Cold War impacted Berlin and what conspired. And then, only if you want, you can come back and catch the beginning of the movie — *The Man from U.N.C.L.E.* — where how the wall stood and movements happened would make more sense through visualization.

Potsdamer Platz is one swanky area in Berlin. It's full of business places, luxury hotels, cafes, IMAX and all the mall like stuff you can ask for. Since the fall of the wall, Potsdamer Platz has got developed into a great commercial place with places like Sony Center and Mall of Berlin and many more. Another place I visited was Zoologischer Garten (Berlin Zoo) in Hardenbergplatz. It also has a commercial place close by for shopping where you get to see the Christmas market, Zoo Palast Berlin where you can catch movies (in German only) and Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church. One can cover these places on foot once you reach the main Zoologischer Garten via any public transport. I used S-Bahn.

Berlin boasts of a super public transport system which is S-Bahn (suburban trains), U-Bahn (underground trains), buses and trams. I use all but trains dominate my commute. I found getting around Berlin by train very convenient. Once you buy a monthly pass for 80 euros, you wouldn't need to punch your daily ticket for a date-time stamp from the kiosk. Seek a train map from any railway personnel from any station (for e.g. Friedrich Str.) and you would be good. Berlin's transit is beautiful and also fun because you get to see the city closely when commuters bring their pets or bikes on board.

I have explored other nice places like Alexanderplatz, Hackescher Market, and West and East Kreuzberg as well. Still some really exciting places in Berlin are yet to be seen like — Friedrichshain, Wedding and Prenzlauer Berg. Berlin is a place with a lot of variety where you can find completely gentrified areas that are hip, cool and gather most of the tourists, areas where you see infinite calm and serenity, areas where an edgy international population resides along with Berliners and has fun. Berlin seems to accept all the kinds and vice versa. It's a relevant place in today's times. People seem to live and let live and extend a lot of value to personal freedom and creativity. It's kind of sexy in an understated, glamorous way. I can't wait to expand my experiences on foot and by train, get to see its nightlife and discover the legend of Berghain by getting denied entry.
